# Yourrest CRM Android 1.0.16 (versionCode 17)

## A changed region or role in 1.0.16

Version 1.0.16 answers the owner's report of 2026-11-25: a marketing officer's account was made on 2026-11-19 with «كل المناطق» and later saved with the five regions ticked one by one. For the app that is another region scope, and 1.0.15 stopped there: it said «تغيرت أذونات الحساب؛ محفوظ على الجهاز 856 نقطة و67 انقطاع من المنطقة السابقة», kept the points, refused «تفعيل التتبع», and the system, which needs tracking, stayed closed to him.

When the server's region scope or role for the signed-in account differs from the one the phone enrolled with and points or gaps wait on the phone, the continuous tracker no longer stops. `ContinuousRouteStore.holdForChangedAccount` moves every queued point, gap and day, unchanged and in one transaction, into the held tables (`held_points`, `held_gaps`, `held_days`, store version 3) under the outbox day each belongs to (its create key, so a day the server already made is found again), with the scope and owner they were captured with; the outbox and its account data are then empty (the last handled system stop is kept), and `refreshAccount` enrolls the new scope at once when the account is a representative who consented, the permissions are granted and the user did not stop the app (otherwise «تفعيل التتبع» does). Enrollment does the same when it meets the old scope first.

Held evidence is uploaded three requests a pass (`drainHeld`, after the outbox and whatever happened to it), only for the owner it was captured for, to days of its own scope: the day the outbox had, or a closed day `/api/mobile-route` makes with `held: true` for a past date only, spanning the group's first to last capture, and only when the account already has a route day of that scope. Today's evidence of an earlier scope without a day waits for tomorrow, and today's still-open day of a former scope takes held evidence only once the new scope's first day has closed it. Wherever the outbox takes a server day (enrollment or a begin), a held group of that day returns first (`adoptDay`). It is never relabeled to the new scope and its points go without their customer binding. When the account comes back to a scope and the server hands the outbox the same open day a held group belongs to, that group returns to the outbox before any new point (`unholdInto`), so the day gets its evidence in capture order. A refusal waits a day, a conflict (409) is asked again and waits a day the third time in a row, a network failure ends the pass, and none of them touches collection, the outbox or the account; only the server's acknowledgment deletes held evidence. Friday evidence is held as quarantined, as in the outbox. The page shows «بيانات موقع من منطقة سابقة لحسابك محفوظة على الهاتف وتُرفع على منطقتها» while some remain (`heldCount`, `heldGapCount`, `heldNotice`, `heldError` in the status). Held evidence of an account that is no longer a representative stays on the phone until it is again. The legacy manual tracker of supervisors and managers is unchanged.

## No more white screen in 1.0.15

Version 1.0.15 answers the owner's report of 2026-11-19: a new Huawei tablet without Google services (no Play Store, no Chrome; its WebView is Huawei's, usually `com.huawei.webview`) installed the app and showed only a white screen. Until 1.0.14 the activity handled no failure of the main page (`onReceivedSslError` only cancelled), so no connection, a TLS failure, a server error, a lost renderer or an engine too old for the site all ended on a blank page.

- **The panel** (`LoadFailurePanel`): a native view over the WebView, not a page, so it shows whatever the page and the engine can do. The reason in Arabic, or in English when the person chose English in the CRM page (the language the app already remembers for its notices), the button «إعادة المحاولة» (it loads the page that failed again, its notification fragment included; a failed `/api/` address loads the first page), and «معلومات للدعم الفني», selectable and left to right: the WebView package, its version and its Chromium, Android with its API level, the maker and model, the app's version and code, the technical error, the page without its query or fragment, and the device's clock with its offset. One screenshot is enough to diagnose. The page under the panel is hidden from accessibility services, gets no touch and gets no bridge.
- **When** (`LoadFailureRules`, `LoadWatch`: pure, checked on the JVM):
  - a main-frame `onReceivedError` on the CRM's address: «تعذّر الاتصال بالنظام. تأكد من الاتصال بالإنترنت ثم أعد المحاولة.» (a failed handshake, `ERROR_FAILED_SSL_HANDSHAKE`, is worded as the security failure);
  - `onReceivedSslError` on the CRM's address while its page is not up: «تعذّر التحقق من أمان الاتصال. تأكد أن تاريخ الجهاز ووقته مضبوطان تلقائيًا، ثم أعد المحاولة.», with the primary error (`SSL_DATE_INVALID`…) and the certificate's validity days beside the device's clock. The handshake is still cancelled; nothing ever calls `proceed`. A later failing resource of a page already shown is cancelled as before, without the panel;
  - a main-frame `onReceivedHttpError` of 500 or more: «النظام لا يستجيب الآن. أعد المحاولة بعد قليل.» (while Android has no network, the connection's words: a service worker with nothing cached answers 503). A 404 or any other 4xx page stays the site's;
  - `onRenderProcessGone`: returns true (the app never crashes for it) and drops that WebView with what waited on it (the bridge, a file chooser, location answers). A renderer the system stopped for memory is recreated on the same page quietly, once a minute; a crash, or a second stop within the minute, shows «توقف محرك عرض الصفحات على هذا الجهاز فجأة. أعد المحاولة.», and «إعادة المحاولة» makes a new WebView;
  - the page never came up: while the app is in front, the page is asked every second (`LoadFailureRules.PROBE`, ES5 so an old engine runs it, reading nothing of the person's data) whether it shows text, or a picture or control with a size; only a document of the CRM's own origin counts (WebView's own error page, still shown while the next page loads, never does), and a navigation the page starts is watched from its start. Still blank 20 s after `onPageFinished`, or 60 s after the start without a finish: «لم تظهر صفحة النظام على هذا الجهاز. أعد المحاولة، وإن تكرر ذلك فحدّث محرك عرض الصفحات (WebView) من متجر التطبيقات (AppGallery أو Google Play).». This panel goes by itself if the page appears within two more minutes (a very slow network);
  - the engine is older than the site, checked before anything loads: Chromium below **111** shows at once «محرك عرض الصفحات (WebView) على هذا الجهاز قديم. حدّثه من متجر التطبيقات (AppGallery أو Google Play) ثم أعد فتح التطبيق.» with «المحرك المثبّت: com.huawei.webview 12.0.0.300 (Chromium 99)، والمطلوب Chromium 111 أو أحدث.», and «المتابعة رغم ذلك» loads the site anyway (for this opening only). The version is the user agent's `Chrome/<major>` (Huawei's WebView carries it too), else the provider's version when it is Chromium's (Google's and AOSP's `114.0.5735.196`); Huawei numbers its own package (`12.0.0.300`), so that is never read as Chromium, and an unknown version is left to the watchdog;
  - no WebView can be made (no engine, or one disabled): the panel «محرك عرض الصفحات غير متاح» instead of a crash.
- **Why 111.** The site is built by vinext on Vite 8 with no `build.target` of its own, so Vite's default `baseline-widely-available` applies to the page's JavaScript: chrome111, edge111, firefox114, safari16.4 (`ESBUILD_BASELINE_WIDELY_AVAILABLE_TARGET` in `vite/dist/node/chunks/logger.js`). vinext sets `build.cssTarget` to chrome111 too, and the styles are Tailwind CSS 4.2, whose colours are `oklch()` and `color-mix()` (Chrome 111). `tsconfig.json`'s `ES2017` is the type checker's, not the build's; the offline shell's own build targets es2020 but uses the same styles. Changing the site's target means changing `LoadFailureRules.MIN_CHROMIUM` with it.
- **What stays the site's.** The service worker (`public/sw.js`) answers a navigation that fails on the network with its offline shell, so WebView reports no error and no panel shows; the shell counts as up once its script draws it, and stays blank (so the panel shows) when its script cannot run. Everything else is unchanged: a notification's page (a tap while the panel shows loads that page, or keeps it for the panel's button behind an old engine), the bridge and its nonce, the download listener, the design files, location and the outbox.

## The camera in 1.0.15

- **Which inputs** (`CameraPhotoRules.offer`): an input that takes a JPEG (`image/*`, `image/jpeg`, `.jpg`/`.jpeg`, any type, or no type named) asks «إضافة صورة أو ملف» with «التقاط صورة بالكاميرا» and «اختيار من الملفات أو المعرض» (the document picker as before, with its multiple selection). That is «صور القالب» (`accept="image/*" multiple`), a competitor's quotation and the mold request's files. An input with `capture` opens the camera at once. The Excel imports, a PDF-only or a PNG-only input keep the picker alone. Backing out of the question gives the page no file.
- **How.** Android's camera app (`ACTION_IMAGE_CAPTURE`) writes the photo into a new empty file of the app's private cache, `cache/camera-photos/photo-<UTC moment>-<8 random hex>.jpg`, through `content://org.yourrest.crm.camera/photo/<name>` (`EXTRA_OUTPUT`, with the write grant on that one URI). There is no AndroidX `FileProvider` in this app, so `CameraPhotoProvider` is a minimal provider of its own: not exported, `grantUriPermissions`, only that folder and that name shape (no other path, no `..`, no link leading out), only the modes `r`, `w`, `wt`, `rw`, `rwt`, and no insert, update or delete; its authority is the literal `org.yourrest.crm.camera` in the manifest (the standalone build links the manifest as written). When the camera answers, the grant is taken back (`revokeUriPermission`) and only then does the page get the photo (one photo; a cancelled camera or an empty file gives none). The WebView reads it through the same provider; «صور القالب» shrinks it itself before uploading.
- **No CAMERA permission.** It is not declared: an app that declares it must hold it at run time before it may even start `ACTION_IMAGE_CAPTURE`, and the camera app needs nothing from this one. No storage permission either. No camera app on the device: «لا يوجد تطبيق كاميرا على هذا الجهاز؛ اختر الصورة من الملفات.» and the picker opens instead.
- **Kept and removed.** A photo stays a day (the page may read it again when the form is saved later); the next start removes older ones and anything else in the folder. If Android ends the app while the camera is open, the page that asked is gone when it answers: the photo is removed and the person takes it again.

Checks: `LoadFailureRulesCheck` (243 JVM checks: the version parsing, which callback covers the page, the deadlines at their edges, an attempt from start to panel and back, the words, the facts, and source guards on `MainActivity` and `LoadFailurePanel`), `CameraPhotoRulesCheck` (152: the inputs, the photo's file and path, the modes, the cleanup, the words, and source and manifest guards), `tests/fixtures/native-camera/CameraPhotoProviderCheck.java` (52 checks of the real `CameraPhotoProvider` on Android stand-ins) and the probe run in JavaScript on stand-in documents, with the earlier checks that guard `MainActivity` and the manifest (`FileChooserCheck`, `DesignFileCheck`, `TripStartCheck`, `MapsLaunchCheck`, `ActivationRecoveryCheck`, `AccountRecoveryCheck`, `OfflineAuthorizationCheck`), all run by `tests/webview-load-android.mjs` (default gate, JDK 17+; with `ANDROID_ALL_JAR` the whole main source is compiled against the pinned framework jar too). `tests/webview-load-probe-browser.mjs` (browser gate, no build) runs the probe in Chromium on the site's own offline pages. Not verified here (no device or emulator): a real WebView of any maker, Huawei's included; a TLS failure, a server error or a lost renderer on a phone; the panel's look on a phone and a tablet; a camera app writing through the provider; the WebView uploading the photo.

## Complaint alerts and the background check in 1.0.14

Version 1.0.14 tells each member of the customer complaints whose step is theirs («شكوى تنتظرك», the owner's item 3 of the GSCO list, 2026-11-16, after GSCO's Android 1.4.9), and checks the alerts in the background even when route tracking is off.

- **What waits for whom** is the server's: `GET /api/complaints/alerts` (every member; no session 401; the screen where the member meets complaints hidden, «الشكاوى» or «الزيارات», 404) lists, from the same queue as the complaints page, the complaints waiting for the signed-in member: the review (the customer-service manager), the correction and the closure (the registrar), the assignment (the sales manager), the delegation (the supervisor), the contact and the resolution (the holder); the administrator only when nobody else is there. At most 20, high importance first, then the page's own order. Each row: id, customer name, category in Arabic and English, importance, the step, whether the holder opened it since it was handed over, and when the step began. No description, demand, note, phone, city or number.
- **The notification** (`ComplaintAlertRules`, `ComplaintAlertFeed`, `ComplaintAlerts`, `ComplaintAlertApi`): channel «شكاوى العملاء» (high importance; the person may mute it), title «شكوى تنتظرك», text «أهمية عالية · <customer> · <category> — بانتظار تكليفك» (the prefix only for high importance), the lock screen «يورست: شكوى تنتظرك» only; in English when the person chose English in the CRM page. One notification per complaint, grouped, high importance sorting first, with a summary «عدد الشكاوى التي تنتظرك: N» when two or more show. A complaint whose step starts again (a reassignment, a return, a reopening) notifies again; one its holder opens, or that no longer waits for the member, is taken away; the same step never notifies twice (ids and stamps kept per account, never the texts). Nothing is announced while the app is in front of the person (the page shows the complaints and their count). 401/403 clear everything; 404 takes the notices away; signing out clears them and cancels the background check; another account on the phone starts with nothing.
- **When the app asks:** on opening and returning (after a minute), every ten minutes from the route tracking service, and from the **background check** (`AlertJobRules`, `AlertCheckJob`): a periodic, persisted JobScheduler job every **15 minutes** (Android's shortest period) with a network constraint, which asks the complaint feed and the customer-service-request feed (`ServiceAlerts`) only inside **06:00 to 18:00 Riyadh, not Friday** (`ContinuousPolicy.inWindow`), **even with route tracking off**. It needs no GPS, starts no service, asks no permission; it cancels itself when the phone holds no session, is cancelled on sign-out and is scheduled again when the app opens or a CRM page loads with a session. Android may batch it later under Doze or a maker's battery rules.
- **A tap** opens the complaint: a cold start loads `#complaint=<id>`, a running page receives `yourrest:open-complaint` (`app/crm-app.tsx`), never replacing a screen with unsaved work; the page opens only a complaint the member's own list holds, on «الشكاوى» or, for the sales team, «الزيارات».
- **Permissions:** none added. A persisted job uses `RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED` and the network constraint `ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE`, both declared since earlier versions; the job service is protected by `BIND_JOB_SERVICE` and not exported. Existing installs grant nothing new; notifications need the permission the app already asks for (Android 13+).

Checks: `ComplaintAlertRulesCheck` (63 JVM checks: the window at its edges, Friday, the 15-minute period, the cadence, which rows notify and which go, the order, the words, the link) and `tests/fixtures/native-complaints/ComplaintAlertsCheck.java` (56 checks of the real `ComplaintAlerts`, `ComplaintAlertFeed` and `AlertCheckJob` on Android stand-ins), with `ServiceAlertCheck` (75) and source guards, all run by `tests/complaint-alerts-android.mjs` (default gate, JDK 17+); with `ANDROID_ALL_JAR` naming the pinned framework jar the whole main source is compiled against it too. Not verified here: a real phone (the notification shade, Doze, JobScheduler's real timing, OEM battery rules) and the APK build itself (it is built on GitHub).

## Nearby factories in 1.0.13

Version 1.0.13 tells a representative when they come near customers they are responsible for that need a visit («تنبيه المصانع القريبة», the owner's item 7 of the GSCO comparison list, 2026-11-16, after GSCO's grouped nearby-project reminder of its Android 1.4.8). The owner's numbers: within **1 km**, customers with **no visit for 60 days or more** and a **saved location on the map**, **one grouped notification**, **the same customer at most once a day**.

- **What qualifies** is the server's: `GET /api/nearby-customers` (the representative only; every other role 403, no session 401, «العملاء» hidden 404) lists the customers assigned to the signed-in representative in their regions, with a saved location (the one «خريطة المصانع» reads), whose latest visit by any member is 60 days old or more (exactly 60 counts), or that were never visited: id, name, coordinates and days since the last visit only. The answer names the member, the role and the regions and is valid ten minutes; the app refuses a list about another account or region, longer than 2000 rows, or with a row it cannot read.
- **When** (`NearbyRules`, pure): only on fixes the continuous route tracking has just accepted and stored (06:00 to 18:00 Riyadh, not Friday; no new GPS request, no new permission), precise to 75 m or better, not older than a minute and not from a mock provider. A customer is "near" when the fix itself is within 1000 m of it (haversine; 999 m in, 1001 m out; the accuracy is not added) at two fixes at least 15 seconds apart (a gap over 90 s, an outside fix or a new day starts again). Every customer confirmed at the same fix goes into one notification, nearest first; 30 seconds at least between two notifications. A customer told once is not told again the same Riyadh day; who was told is saved per member (ids and the day only, SHA-256 keyed) before the notification shows, so a restart or a crash never repeats one. A new day tells again.
- **The list** (`NearbyCustomers`, `NearbyApi`): asked at the first fix, every five minutes while fixes arrive, and again just before a notification unless it came in the last minute, so a customer visited or reassigned meanwhile is not told; never two requests within 15 s; a failure is tried again after a minute; 401, 403 and 404 drop the list, take the shown notices away and wait five minutes. Logout, an account or region change and the tracking's own `disable` forget the list and the shown notices; the 18:00 pause and a stopped service drop the list and the evidence but keep who was told today. A new process takes the shown notices away until the server answers again (as GSCO).
- **The notification**: channel «المصانع القريبة» (default importance; the person may mute it), title «مصنع قريب منك بحاجة إلى زيارة» / «مصنعان قريبان…» / «3 مصانع قريبة…» / «11 مصنعًا قريبًا…», each line «<name> — على بعد 420 م · آخر زيارة قبل 75 يومًا» (or «لم تُسجَّل له زيارة»), the lock screen «يورست: مصانع قريبة بحاجة إلى زيارة» only; in English when the person chose English in the CRM page (read from the trusted page's own `localStorage` «yr-language» when a page loads and when the app goes to the background). It goes by itself after ten minutes, or at 18:00 if sooner. A tap opens «خريطة المصانع» on the nearest customer: a cold start loads `#nearby=<id>`, a running page receives `yourrest:open-nearby-customer` and never replaces a screen with unsaved work; the page opens only a customer its own data has (the customer's card when the map is hidden from the member).
- **Permissions:** none added. It needs what continuous tracking already holds (precise and background location, notifications on Android 13+). Existing installs grant nothing new.

Checks: `NearbyRulesCheck` (287 JVM checks) and `tests/fixtures/native-nearby/NearbyCustomersCheck.java` (72 checks of the real `NearbyCustomers` on Android stand-ins), both run by `tests/nearby-customers-android.mjs` (default gate, JDK 17+). The full main source also compiles with `javac --release 17` against the pinned Android 35 framework jar (`android-all-15-robolectric-12650502.jar`, the one `tools/build-oss.py` uses) with a stub `R` class. Not verified here: a real phone (GPS, the notification shade, Doze, OEM battery rules), the APK build itself (it is built on GitHub).


## Updates inside the app

The CRM shows an Arabic/English update banner at login and throughout the app.
It checks on opening, returning and reconnecting. Existing older APKs start their
first update through the banner and their existing browser download handler;
administrators do not distribute APK files to individual users. Version 1.0.10
adds private native downloads with progress, retry and Android installation
confirmation. It verifies size, SHA-256, package name, increasing version code
and the original installed signing certificate before opening PackageInstaller.
Android may request permission to install updates from Yourrest. Installation
requires user confirmation; this is not silent installation. Save any open form
before installing. Existing saved records, offline outboxes and login remain in
place because the signed package updates the existing app without uninstalling.

Public update metadata is generated from the verified APK build report using
`scripts/publish-android-release.mjs`, then served without caching at
`/api/app-update`. Do not change the APK without regenerating this metadata.

Native Android 8+ container for `https://crm.yourrest.org`, targeting API 35. Install the matching CRM server update before this APK. The Android package and signing identity remain unchanged from earlier releases.

## Design files in 1.0.12

Version 1.0.12 lets a representative work with a mold request's design files («ملفات التصميم») inside the app, without the browser:

- **Choosing files.** The file picker follows the page's `accept` list (`FileChooserRules`). An input that names no types keeps the old list (images, PDF, CSV, Excel); MIME types are kept and known extensions (pdf, png, jpg/jpeg, webp, gif, svg, csv, xlsx, xls, doc/docx, txt, zip) become MIME types. When any entry has no type Android's picker can filter by (DWG, DXF, STEP/STP, IGS/IGES, STL, SLDPRT/SLDASM/SLDDRW, NC1, DSTV, AI, EPS, CDR, PSD, RAR, 7Z, ...) the picker shows every file; the page and the server still check extension, size and content.
- **Opening («عرض») and saving («تنزيل»).** Only `https://crm.yourrest.org/api/mold-requests/design-file?...` is handled (`DesignFileRules`); every other link and download keeps its earlier behaviour. A main-frame navigation to it (the «عرض» link) is intercepted so the page stays. A tap on its «تنزيل» link (`<a download>`) is handed to the app by the script the app injects into the trusted page (`designFile` on the nonce-gated bridge), so the progress dialog shows at once and WebView never starts its own download; the download listener still takes a design-file download that reaches it (a link tapped before the page finished loading). The app fetches the same URL natively, one file at a time and off the UI thread, sending the WebView session cookie for that origin and the app's user agent, without following redirects, with a 130-second read timeout (production may take 120 s), a ten-minute overall limit and a hard cap of 26 MiB (on Content-Length and while reading). The name comes from Content-Disposition (`filename*` UTF-8 first, else `filename`) and is made safe (no path separators, control or direction characters or leading dots; at most 180 UTF-8 bytes with the extension kept; otherwise `design-file`).
- «عرض» (a navigation, or `inline=1`) writes the file to the private cache folder `cache/design-files/<random>/` and opens it with `ACTION_VIEW`, the answer's MIME type only when it is one the CRM serves (`application/pdf`, `image/png`, `image/jpeg`, `image/gif`, `image/webp`, as `moldDesignContentType`), otherwise `application/octet-stream` (so an answer can never name an APK, HTML or script type), and a `content://org.yourrest.crm.designfiles/...` URI from the app's own read-only `DesignFileProvider` (not exported, per-URI read grant only, serves only that folder, guarded against path traversal). If no app can open the type (for example DWG) the app says so and offers «حفظ». Another app receives a file only after a tap: a navigation WebView reports without a user gesture (a script or a redirect), or a download that asks to open, first asks «فتح الملف في تطبيق آخر على الجهاز؟».
- «تنزيل» asks where to save with the system picker (`ACTION_CREATE_DOCUMENT`, no storage permission on any Android version, the same type rule), writes the bytes there with mode `wt` (from Android 10 `w` does not truncate an overwritten longer file; `w` only for a provider that refuses `wt`) and shows «تم حفظ الملف». Cancelling the picker saves nothing. After a failed write only a document this save created (empty when the picker returned it) is removed, never a file the person chose to overwrite. The copy waiting for the picker survives a recreated activity (dark mode, language, split screen, or the process ending meanwhile); if it was lost anyway, the empty document is removed and the person is told to tap «تنزيل» again.
- Errors (on the app's own request: «عرض», and «تنزيل» tapped on a loaded page): 401 reads «انتهت جلسة الدخول؛ سجّل الدخول مرة أخرى»; any other non-200 answer shows the CRM's own Arabic `{"error": ...}` text, else a general message; a lost connection reads «تعذّر تنزيل الملف؛ تحقق من الاتصال». A download that WebView itself started (the listener fallback) reaches the app only when WebView's request succeeded: WebView drops a failed one (401, 404, 429, 5xx, offline) without a word.
- Cached copies older than a day are removed when the app starts. The session cookie is never logged, never put in an intent, and never given to another app or the system DownloadManager. No Android permission is added.

The provider's authority is the literal `org.yourrest.crm.designfiles` in `AndroidManifest.xml` because `tools/build-oss.py` links the manifest as written (no `${applicationId}` placeholder). A Gradle debug build (`org.yourrest.crm.dev`) declares the same authority, so it cannot be installed next to the release app on one phone.

Checks: `FileChooserCheck` (accept-list rules and picker source guards) and `DesignFileCheck` (link allowlist, inline flag, Content-Disposition names, size cap, error wording, provider path guard, plus source and manifest guards). A «تنزيل» tap on a loaded page costs one request; only the listener fallback costs two (WebView reads the answer's headers before it hands the download to the app, then the app fetches the file). Not verified here: the injected «تنزيل» tap handler and WebView's gesture flag in a real WebView, opening and saving on a real phone, viewer apps, the save picker, and very slow networks.

## Customer-service request notifications in 1.0.11

Version 1.0.11 tells a representative when customer service assigns a request to them, even while Yourrest is closed or the phone is locked. The tracking foreground service (working hours: 06:00 to 18:00 Asia/Riyadh, never Friday) asks `GET /api/service-queue/alerts` every ten minutes through the signed-in session; opening or returning to the app asks at most once a minute. The server answers with the requests that wait for that member personally: identifier, customer name, a one-line need, what is expected (first contact, assign, delegate or approve), whether it was already opened and when it began waiting. No registration text, attachment, note, phone number or location is part of the answer, and the session cookie never leaves native memory.

A request is announced once per start (`id@since`); a re-assignment is a new start. Announced keys are kept per member in private preferences for 30 days (at most 300) and another member signing in on the same phone starts clean. `ServiceAlertPolicy` also skips requests already opened, skips announcing while the person is looking at the app (the page already shows the request) and folds more than three new requests into one summary. The lock screen shows only a generic title; the customer and need appear after unlocking. The channel is "طلبات خدمة العملاء" (high importance); on Android 13+ the notification permission is the one the tracking setup already asks for. Tapping a notification opens that request in the CRM: a running page receives `yourrest:open-service-case`, a cold start loads `#service-case=ID`, and a screen with unsaved work is never replaced.

Managers and supervisors, who do not run the tracking service, see the same requests in the Visits badge, panel and banner when they open the app or the website; this release has no background polling for them. Installed apps older than 1.0.11 keep working and receive requests in the page without the phone notification.

Not verified here: delivery on a real phone, Doze and battery-optimisation behaviour, and notification permission prompts on Android 13+. Test on company phones before relying on it.

## Offline route recovery in 1.0.9

Update both the server and each phone APK. The website update alone cannot repair native offline collection. Pending point timestamps, owner identity, day and selected-customer binding remain the original captured values, including after delayed delivery. Server replay accepts authorized historical observations without a fixed age cutoff, while keeping account-creation, schedule, ownership, current scope, device and timestamp checks. Do not uninstall the app or clear its storage while data is pending: either action deletes Android app-private storage.

## User-initiated dictation

Version 1.0.8 adds speech-to-text through an installed Android speech recognition activity, separately from location setup. Tap a field microphone, speak Arabic or English, review the inserted text and save the visit normally. No audio is archived by CRM. An available device speech service is required; if unavailable, use the keyboard microphone or type. The website exposes a clear message on a microphone attempt in an older APK rather than silently invoking an unsupported WebView API.

The trusted bridge advertises `speechProtocol:1` and `speechSupported`. `speechStart({sessionId,language})` returns `{sessionId,text,cancelled}`. `speechCancel({sessionId})` invalidates that exact session, including while the external recognition activity is in front. Recognition is bounded to 75 seconds and 5,000 text characters. Navigation, logout, a changed authenticated-session fingerprint, a changed field/customer, or a disabled/closed form prevents late text from entering another context. No microphone or foreground-service permission is added to CRM for this external recognition flow.

Use the in-app update banner to install the same-signed current APK over Yourrest. Publishing an update makes it discoverable; Android still asks the user to confirm installation. Actual microphone recognition still needs physical-device acceptance testing; browser mocks and native session checks are not that test.

## Representative continuous mode

The authenticated representative activates this mode through the visible setup button. `prepare({continuous:true})` shows a new, explicit Arabic disclosure: location, movement and stops are collected only from **06:00 inclusive to 18:00 exclusive in Asia/Riyadh, Saturday through Thursday (no Friday collection)**, with the screen locked or another app open during that period. Device timezone changes do not change the permitted hours. Existing broader consent remains valid for this reduced collection period. It asks for precise location and notifications, then separately explains Android's **Allow all the time** location setting. Earlier visit-only consent does not activate this policy.

`enroll({memberId})` verifies the actual signed-in server member, representative role, region scope and device before persisting enrollment and starting the location foreground service. No selected customer is required. A visible Android notification describes the 06:00–18:00 collection period excluding Friday, and explicitly says collection is paused outside that period. The representative has no app-provided End workday action in this mode; Android's own permission and stop controls remain effective. Logout disables enrollment and the cached offline authorization immediately. Pending points and gaps retain their original owner on the serialized worker; they can be uploaded only after that same account authenticates online again. An empty old-account store can be released for a different account. Pending evidence is never transferred to another account.

The native receiver handles `BOOT_COMPLETED` and `MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED` with stored enrollment, explicit consent, precise/background location and notifications. Time/timezone changes and a single ordinary inexact boundary alarm re-evaluate the schedule for an existing service; these alarms never start an otherwise stopped service or bypass Android foreground-start restrictions. The service attempts current server authentication before collecting on a restart and after an overnight pause. A successful authenticated response grants a local seven-day collection lease bound to the same member, representative role and region scope. A retryable connection failure or temporary server response can use the still-valid lease with current consent and OS permissions; first activation always needs the server. Explicit authentication/scope rejection (including HTTP 401, 403 and 409) and TLS failure never authorize this fallback. An expired or absent lease waits for successful online verification. This is a bounded permission to collect locally, not permission to upload under stale server credentials. The server always checks current authorization when data is uploaded.

Android can suspend or stop an app, remove permissions, restrict its battery use, or shut down the device. System Force stop is respected. On supported versions the next process checks `ApplicationExitInfo.REASON_USER_REQUESTED` and blocks automatic restart after an explicit system stop until the user reactivates enrollment. There are no stealth collection mechanisms, accessibility services, device-owner mechanisms or exact-alarm restart loops. The visible foreground service remains alive during scheduled pauses to support permitted resumption without new alarm permissions. Its location subscriptions are removed while paused, including the whole of Friday. Thursday at 18:00 schedules the next collection window for Saturday at 06:00. A main-thread boundary timer and an ordinary inexact wakeup alarm re-evaluate the schedule; callback guards independently reject every out-of-window fix even if Android delays delivery of a timer or alarm.

## Collection and storage

During the permitted hours, GPS and network updates are requested every 15 seconds with a zero-distance threshold in continuous mode, so stationary observations remain real samples. Android decides the actual delivery cadence. Each valid callback is saved directly in app-private SQLite before upload work, retaining its real timestamp, accuracy, UUID and customer binding captured at that callback. Network failures cannot hold accepted samples only in a worker's RAM queue.

Continuous data is partitioned by Riyadh date in a separate private outbox, with stable begin keys per day and a 100,000-pending-point capacity. There is no automatic age-based deletion or 48-hour upload deadline. At capacity, the store preserves all pending samples, records a buffer_full gap where possible and refuses new samples until capacity is available; storage or device failures cannot be treated as successful recording. Points and gap events upload chronologically in batches of at most 50 points. Acknowledgments are checked against the requested day, member and device, and only acknowledged sent IDs are removed. HTTP work is coalesced; each scheduled pass and the enrolled app-resume sync upload at most one chronological batch or gap before yielding the serialized worker to foreground actions. Explicit bridge flush and authorization retain their existing larger budgets. Concurrent inserts do not confuse upload acknowledgment counts. Territory changes or revoked authentication disable collection and preserve pending evidence for authorized resolution; they never silently clear it. Other rejected/conflicting data remains visible for review instead of being silently lost. Benign same-account conflicts receive at most three status-verified retries.

At 18:00 collection pauses without fabricating a GPS exit, visit duration, gap or day-end event. No new location is subscribed to or persisted outside the permitted window. Pre-upgrade Friday points and gaps remain unmodified in the private outbox as quarantined evidence without automatic age deletion and still counted toward capacity; they do not enter upload selectors or block a later permitted date. Queued totals still protect account identity and storage limits; pending upload counts include only eligible dates. A separate informational `quarantineNotice` and `quarantinedCount`/`quarantinedGapCount` report these rows, without setting a collection error or `syncError`. Already queued in-window points can upload at any time, including after 18:00 and on Friday; the restriction is on when locations are captured, not the later delivery time. The following eligible 06:00 window attempts authenticated verification before subscribing again, with the bounded offline lease fallback described above. Each new Saudi date clears yesterday's customer binding. Historical queued points and gaps are uploaded before the new date is opened when possible; the backend also preserves valid late evidence around a system midnight closure. New day boundaries never fabricate movement, customer arrival or departure. The reports derive stops from actual verified fixes and show gaps when evidence is missing.

Legacy visit-mode outbox data is migrated after verifying the same representative. IDs and dates remain unchanged, and data is copied durably before its legacy acknowledgment/removal. Managers and supervisors retain the existing optional visit/day tracking behavior; they are never automatically enrolled in background collection. Their optional collector also removes provider subscriptions and rejects captures outside the same working-hours window.

## Web bridge and navigation

The user agent contains `YourrestCRM-Android/1`. The website must suppress its browser collector as soon as this marker is present. A trusted main-page load injects `window.__yourrestNative.request(method,payload)` and emits `yourrest:native-ready`; state changes emit `yourrest:native-state` with the state as `detail`.

Methods:

- `prepare({continuous?:boolean})`: informed consent and Android permission setup only.
- `enroll({memberId})`: verify and activate/resume representative continuous mode.
- `start({memberId,stopId})`: bind the selected authenticated customer after verifying the current server member, scope, device and active stop. It does not drain unrelated queued observations first; their original captured customer bindings remain unchanged. Collection remains active between visits during the working-hours window.
- `status({})`, `flush({})`, `logout({})`.
- `stop({})`: legacy day mode only; rejects while continuous enrollment is active.
- `directions({memberId,stopId,latitude,longitude})`: requires a trusted foreground page, the bound member and stop, running tracking (or scheduled pause with valid enrollment), and exact coordinates from the server's customer snapshot. After that preflight, opens a fixed `google.navigation:q=latitude,longitude&mode=d` intent pinned to `com.google.android.apps.maps`, without the BROWSABLE category. If Maps is unavailable or the OS rejects it, attempts the canonical HTTPS Google Maps directions URL once. It does not use `resolveActivity`, which can misreport installed apps under package visibility rules. If both launches fail, a native dialog explains the failure and the bridge returns `maps_launch_failed:` with that human-readable message; binding/permission/coordinate failures retain their original error and never get this marker.

Successful requests return `{tracking,permission,pendingCount,pendingGapCount,lastFixAt,accuracy,error,dayId,date,stopId,memberId,directionsSupported,mapsLaunchSupported,appVersion,continuousSupported,enrolled,backgroundPermission,workHoursSupported,fridayExclusionSupported,workWeekSupported,workingDays,workingHoursTimeZone,trackingWindowActive,trackingWindowStart,trackingWindowEnd,trackingTimeZone,nextTrackingWindowAt}`. `appVersion` comes from the installed Android package metadata; `mapsLaunchSupported:true` identifies the explicit Maps launcher. Both pending counts are integers from the durable store: point count and gap-event count. A points-only zero does not prove the outbox is empty; the website must preserve its preselection sync barrier when gaps remain or the legacy gap count is unknown. A day ID may be null before the first permitted window or during an offline rollover. Outside working hours, valid enrollment returns `enrolled:true,tracking:false,error:null`. The `workHoursSupported:true` capability identifies this APK policy so the website can distinguish older 24-hour clients and prompt an update. `fridayExclusionSupported:true` and `workWeekSupported:true` identify this Friday-excluding version; `workingDays:[0,1,2,3,4,6]` uses JavaScript weekday numbers (Friday is 5), and `workingHoursTimeZone` is `Asia/Riyadh`. Earlier APKs omit the capability flags; the website must check them before invoking new commands. Except for cancellation of a matching speech session and the existing bounded Maps handoff, a valid trusted bridge request received while the activity is hidden rejects immediately with an instruction to open the app, rather than waiting for the 90-second bridge timeout. Nonce and trusted-origin checks still run before any reply.

Async ordinary directions links (including outside tracking hours) can use the same launcher from the currently trusted CRM main frame. This exception accepts only HTTPS `www.google.com/maps/dir/` URLs with `api=1`, one finite in-range coordinate pair, `travelmode=driving` and `dir_action=navigate`, with no extra or duplicate parameters, userinfo, arbitrary port or fragment. It requires a genuine gesture or the current nonempty injected navigation nonce, while the activity is resumed. This preserves the legitimate handoff when WebView no longer reports a gesture after an awaited CRM request. Subframes and other external URLs retain the ordinary gesture requirement. The adapter constructs both intents from validated coordinates; it never launches a supplied arbitrary intent or custom scheme.

WebView authentication stays in the cookie jar. Native requests obtain current HttpOnly cookies directly through `CookieManager`; cookies never enter JavaScript or the outbox. The low-level bridge requires a per-main-navigation nonce and exact trusted HTTPS page. Mixed HTTP, file URLs, SSL bypass, third-party cookies and WebView debugging are disabled. Ordinary external navigation still requires a user gesture. File uploads use Android's document picker, following the input's accept list since 1.0.12; design files open and save inside the app (see "Design files in 1.0.12"); other browser-generated downloads still instruct the user to use the browser.

The code has compiled against the Android 35 API classes.
